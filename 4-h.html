What I did

  • Helped rebuild the Middlesex Rabbit Club after years of being inactive. It restarted near the six-member minimum and now has about 20 members.
  • Plan and lead meetings, organize educational programming, coordinate rabbit shows, and arrange facilities, guest speakers and judges.
  • Teach showmanship, mentor younger members, and make sure members without a rabbit can take part fully.
  • Youth Leader of the Middlesex Innovation and Entrepreneurship Club in 2026.
  • 4-H Ontario delegate to the 4-H Canada National Members Forum in Regina, November 21 to 26, 2026, one of eight from the province.
  • Attended the 4-H Ontario Impact Conference in Oakville in October 2025.
  • Received the South Lobo Women’s Institute Scholarship, $250, for my 4-H work and leadership.
  • Shows and events include Middlesex County Achievement Day, Glencoe Fair, Lucknow Invitational, the Komoka Rabbit Breeders Show and the Western Fair.

In my words

Rabbits was the first 4-H club I ever joined. Later I walked into a dairy club without a cow of my own. They gave me one to show, and I ended up winning Reserve Champion.

That is why I make room for members who don’t own an animal. A lot of kids, especially in the city, never get a way into agriculture. Rabbits can be that way in, because you can borrow one or keep one in your house.
